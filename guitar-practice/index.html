Set up a practice loop in 30 seconds

  1. Copy the YouTube URL of the lesson or song and paste it into repeat-videos.com. It starts looping automatically.
  2. Drag the A handle to where the riff starts and the B handle to where it ends. Only that section repeats now.
  3. Drop the speed to 0.5× or 0.75×. The pitch stays the same, so you play along in the original key.
  4. As it gets clean, step the speed back up — 0.75×, 1×, and 1.25× for overspeed practice.

Why looping beats scrubbing back by hand

  • No interruptions — the section repeats seamlessly, so you stay in playing position instead of reaching for the mouse every 20 seconds.
  • Honest rep counting — the play counter shows how many times the loop actually ran. Fifty clean repetitions is a fact, not a feeling.
  • Loops that persist — A-B points and speed are saved per video. Tomorrow's session starts exactly where today's ended.
  • Shareable sections — send your student or bandmate a link that opens the same solo at the same bars and the same speed.

Practice ideas that work well with loops

Slow-to-fast laddering

Loop a two-bar phrase at 0.5×, play it ten clean times, raise to 0.75×, repeat, then 1×. The counter keeps you honest at every step.

Problem-bar isolation

Tighten the A-B window to just the transition that keeps failing — often only one or two seconds — and drill exactly that, not the comfortable bars around it.

Ear training and transcription

Loop a lick at 0.5× and pick it out by ear before looking at the tab. Slower playback keeps pitches identical, so what you hear is what you play.

FAQ

How do I loop one part of a guitar video?

Drag the A and B handles on the timeline around the section. Playback repeats between them until you move the handles or turn the loop off.

Does slowing down change the pitch?

No — playback is time-stretched, notes keep their pitch at any speed from 0.5× to 2×.

Do I need an account?

No. Everything works as a guest. Signing in only adds syncing your saved songs and loops across devices.
